Everything in Drive is private to you until you share it. You can share a file or folder with specific people, with
a whole team, or with anyone who has the link, and decide whether they can view it or also make changes.

## Share with specific people

1. Find the file or folder in Drive.
2. Right-click it (or open the **⋮** menu), hover over **Share**, then click **Share** in the sub-menu that appears.
3. In the **Share** dialog, type a name or email address in the box at the top, then pick the person. They appear
   under **People with access**.
4. Choose what they can do from the dropdown next to their name: **Editor** (view and make changes) or **Viewer**
   (view only). New people are added as **Editors**.
5. Click **Save**.

To remove someone later, open the same dialog, set their dropdown to **Remove**, and click **Save**.

## Share with a team

If you belong to a team, click **Share with team** at the bottom of the dialog and pick the team. Everyone in
that team gets access.

## Share with a link

Under **General access**, a file starts as **Restricted**. Only the people you've added can open it.

To share more widely, click the lock icon to switch it to **Unrestricted**, then choose **Can view** or
**Can edit**. Use the **Copy link** button to copy the link and send it to whoever you like.

<div class="eigen-callout">

An **Unrestricted** link still requires the other person to sign in to Eigen. It isn't open to the public web.
There's no anonymous, sign-in-free sharing.

</div>

## Let people know

In the **Share** dialog, click the **Email collaborators** button to open a compose window where you can write a
message to everyone you've shared with. Eigen includes a link to the file automatically. The button is disabled
while you have unsaved changes, so save first if you've just added someone.

## Control who can change sharing

If you own the file, you can stop editors from re-sharing it. In the **Share** dialog, turn off **Editors can
share**. With it off, only you can add or remove people. Editors can still work on the file, but they can't change
who else has access.
