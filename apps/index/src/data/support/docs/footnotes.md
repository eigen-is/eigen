A footnote puts a small number in your text and a note with the same number at the end of the page. Use one for a source, a remark, or a detail that would break up the sentence.

## Add a footnote

1. Click in your text where the number should go. With text selected, the number goes after the selection.
2. Open the **Insert** menu and choose **Page elements → Footnote**, or press **⌘⌥F** on a Mac (**Ctrl+Alt+F** on Windows and Linux).
3. Type the note. Your cursor is already in it, at the end of the page.
4. Press **Esc** to go back to your text, right after the number. Clicking in your text works too.

Footnotes number themselves 1, 2, 3 in the order they appear. Add one before another and the numbers after it move up.

## Edit a footnote

Click the note at the end of the page, or click its number in your text, and type. The toolbar and the **Format** and **Insert** menus now work on the note, so you can make text bold or add a link. **⌘B**, **⌘I**, and **⌘U** (**Ctrl+B**, **Ctrl+I**, and **Ctrl+U**) work as in your text. A note holds paragraphs of text: no headings, lists, tables, or another footnote.

**⌘Z** (**Ctrl+Z**) in a note undoes your last change, in the note or in your text, as one history.

## Remove a footnote

Put your cursor right after the number in your text and press **Backspace**. The number and its note go together. Copying or cutting a sentence with a number takes its note along, and pasting it brings the note back.

## Good to know

- Making text across a number bold, colored, or a comment leaves the note as it is, and so does **Clear formatting**.
- The find bar searches inside footnotes. A match in one opens that note, with the match selected.
- **Word count** doesn't count footnotes.
- Comments can't go on the text of a note.
- On a phone you can read footnotes, but you can't add or change them. Only people who can edit the document can.
