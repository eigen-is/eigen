You can insert images, charts, and tables anywhere in a document. Images can come from your device or from Drive. Charts start with sample data you then edit. Tables start as a 3×3 grid and you can grow them from there.

## Insert an image

### On a wide screen

1. Place your cursor where you want the image to appear.
2. Click the **Insert image** button in the toolbar (the image icon with a plus symbol).
3. The **Insert image** dialog opens. Choose where to get the image from:
   - Click **Upload from device** to pick a file from your computer.
   - Or browse your Drive and click a file, then click **Select**.
4. The image appears in the document at the cursor position.

You can also drag an image file from your computer and drop it straight onto the document.

### From the Insert menu

This works on any screen.

1. Place your cursor where you want the image.
2. Click **Insert** in the toolbar.
3. Choose **Image**.
4. The **Insert image** dialog opens. Choose **Upload from device** to pick a file, or browse Drive and click **Select**.

### Paste an image

If you have an image on the clipboard (copied from another app or from elsewhere in the document), paste it with Ctrl+V (or ⌘V on Mac). The image is inserted at the cursor.

## Resize and position an image

Click an image to select it. Resize handles appear at its corners. Drag a handle to change the size. The height adjusts to keep the proportions. To stretch an image instead, uncheck **Keep aspect ratio** in the **Image** panel: then a handle on any edge or corner changes the width and height separately. To resize with the keyboard, select the image and hold Shift while you press an arrow key: Right or Up makes it wider, Left or Down makes it narrower. To place the cursor before or after an image that sits on its own line, click in the empty space to its left or right.

On a wide screen, selecting an image also opens the **Image** panel on the right side of the editor. From there you can:

- **Width** and **Height**: type the image's width or height in centimeters and press Enter. While **Keep aspect ratio** is checked, the other side follows. Uncheck it to stretch the image. Each side you type is at least 2.65 cm, and the image is no wider than the text (half of it for a floating image, the width of the paper for an image behind or in front of the text); a wider size shrinks to fit, keeping its shape.
- **Keep aspect ratio**: checked for an image when you select it. Unchecking it lasts while the image stays selected.
- **Style**: set the image to sit on its own line (the default), float to the left, or float to the right with text wrapping around it, or place it behind or in front of the text (see below). An image wider than half the text shrinks to half when it starts to float, so the text has room beside it.
- **Align**: when the image is on its own line, choose left, center, or right alignment.
- **Alt**: add alternative text, which is used by screen readers and shown if the image fails to load.
- **Cap**: add a caption that appears beneath the image. An image behind or in front of the text has no caption.
- **Replace image**: swap the image for a different file from your device or Drive, keeping the same position in the document. The new image takes its own size and shape.

**Format → Image** has **Replace image** and the same styles: **In line**, **Wrap left**, **Wrap right**, **Behind text**, and **In front of text**. It works on any screen, a phone included. The **Image** item is greyed out until you select an image.

## Put an image behind or in front of the text

An image behind or in front of the text sits anywhere on the page you drag it to, and the text runs over it or under it, without making room.

1. Click the image to select it.
2. In the **Image** panel, click **Behind text** or **In front of text** under **Style**. Or choose **Format → Image**, then **Behind text** or **In front of text**.
3. Drag the image to where you want it. It stays on the paper, so you can drag it into the margins but not off the page.

The image stays where it was when you switch. It belongs to the paragraph it sits on, so it moves down when you type above that paragraph, and it goes when you delete the paragraph. Drop it over another paragraph to attach it there. Indenting its paragraph moves the image along with it.

To select an image in front of the text, click it. To select one behind the text, click a part of it that no text covers, or hold Alt (Option on a Mac) and click it. Once it's selected, you can drag it or its handles even where text covers it. On a phone, drag a selected image with your finger. A finger on an image that isn't selected scrolls the page, and an image fully under text can't be selected there.

An image behind or in front of the text can't be selected with the keyboard, and the arrow keys don't move it. With the image selected, Shift and an arrow key still resize it.

An image in a table, a footnote, or a header or footer can't go behind or in front of the text, so the **Image** panel and **Format → Image** don't offer it there. One you paste there sits on a line of its own. Drop an image behind or in front of the text over a table and it stays attached to the paragraph it was on.

In a PDF or a printout, an image near the end of a page can be cut off at the paper's edge, or left out, since it doesn't continue on the next page. An image whose paragraph runs over two pages prints on the first.

## Download an image

Right-click an image and choose **Download original image**. You get the image file at its full size and in its own format. This works with view access too.

The same menu has **Cut**, **Copy**, and **Delete** for the image, and **Add comment**, to comment on the image itself. With view access it has **Copy**. See [Comment and discuss](/support/docs/comments).

## Add a chart

1. Place your cursor where you want the chart.
2. Click **Insert** in the toolbar, choose **Chart**, then **Bar**, **Column**, **Line**, or **Pie**. A bar chart has horizontal bars, a column chart vertical ones.
3. The chart appears at the cursor with sample data, and stays selected.

Drag a handle to resize the chart. Its width and height change on their own, so you can make it wider without making it taller. While you drag, the size shows in centimeters. To keep the chart's shape, check **Keep aspect ratio** in the **Chart** panel: then only the corner handles show, and they keep the proportions.

On a wide screen, selecting a chart opens the **Chart** panel on the right side of the editor. It starts with the chart's **Width** and **Height** in centimeters and **Keep aspect ratio**, unchecked for a chart, which work as they do for an image. Then **Style** and **Align** place the chart as they place an image, behind or in front of the text included, and **Cap** adds a caption beneath it, unless the chart is behind or in front of the text. Below those, the panel sets the **Type**, the **Stacking** of a bar or column chart (**None**, **Standard** or **100%**), the **Title**, where the **Legend** goes, the **Font** of all the chart's text, each axis's **Title** and **Show axis**, the category axis's **Reverse axis order**, the **Min**, **Max**, and **Gridlines** of the axis with the numbers, and the color of each series, or of each slice of a pie chart. **Min** and **Max** pick their own values while they're empty and are percents on a **100%** stack; switching **Stacking** to or from **100%** empties them. A color reads **Automatic** until you pick one; **Automatic** in a picked color's list puts it back. Below them, **Labels** sets the **Color** of all the chart's text. **Fill** sets the chart's background, and its **Style** how the bars and slices are drawn: **Solid**, **Hachure**, **Cross-hatch**, or **Zigzag**. Solid bars and slices have no outline; hatched ones get an outline in their own color. **Stroke** sets the color, width, and style of the axes and gridlines, and the width of a line chart's lines, except in a chart imported from Office, which keeps its own, and **Sketch** how rough the chart looks, from **Architect** (clean) to **Cartoonist**. A new chart starts clean and solid, like a Google Docs chart. A chart with more than 200 values (for a pie, more than 200 slices) draws its bars, lines, and slices smooth and solid whatever the sketch, so it stays quick to show; the **Sketch** section says so.

### Edit the numbers

1. Double-click the chart, or click **Edit data** in the **Chart** panel.
2. The **Edit data** dialog shows the data as a small spreadsheet: categories down column A, series across row 1, and the category axis title in A1. Click a cell and type to replace it, or double-click it (or press **F2**) to change part of it. Press **Enter** to move down or **Tab** to move right. **Escape** cancels what you typed in a cell. A cell left empty leaves a gap in the chart.
3. Type in an empty row or column after the data to add a category or a series. To remove one, click its row number or column letter and press **Delete**. **Delete** on selected cells empties them.
4. Click **Save** to update the chart, or **Cancel** to leave it as it was.

You can paste cells copied from a spreadsheet or a table in a document into the grid, and copy cells out of it. A paste fills the grid from the selected cell, and the grid grows to fit. A chart holds up to 12 series, 200 categories, and 2,000 values; when a paste has more, the dialog says how much it pasted and leaves the rest out. Numbers may have thousands separators, a currency sign or a percent sign, as a spreadsheet shows them.

You can copy a chart from Sheets, Slides, or Vector and paste it into a document, where it stays a chart you can edit. A chart wider than the page shrinks to fit. Headers, footers, and footnotes don't take a chart pasted from another app. Copying a chart in a document and pasting it into Sheets, Slides, or Vector works the same way. A chart keeps its look wherever you paste it.

Right-click a chart and choose **Download image** to save it as an SVG picture on a white background, named after its title. This works with view access too. The same menu has **Cut**, **Copy**, and **Delete**, and with view access **Copy**.

A chart prints and downloads as a PDF or a web page in its look. In a Word file it is a Word chart with its numbers, kept the way Word keeps a chart's data. A sketched, hatched, or dashed look is drawn plain, and a gradient background becomes its middle color. See [Export to Word, PDF, or HTML](/support/docs/export).

## Insert a table

1. Place your cursor where you want the table.
2. Click **Insert** in the toolbar.
3. Choose **Table**. A 3×3 table with a header row is inserted straight away.

## Edit a table

Click inside the table. The **Table** panel opens on the right side of the editor (on a wide screen).

The same actions are under **Format → Table**, which works on any screen, a phone included: **Insert row above**, **Insert row below**, **Insert column left**, **Insert column right**, **Delete row**, **Delete column**, **Delete table**, **Distribute columns**, **Merge cells**, **Unmerge cells**, **Toggle header row**, **Cell background color**, **All borders**, **Outer borders**, and **No borders**. The **Table** item is greyed out until your cursor is in a table.

Right-click a cell for the same actions in one menu. It starts with **Cut**, **Copy**, and **Paste**, offers **Add comment** when you have text selected, and ends with **Delete row**, **Delete column**, and **Delete table** in red. The first time you choose **Paste**, your browser may ask to let the page see your clipboard. This menu needs edit access, and works in a header or footer while you edit it.

**Add or remove columns**

Under **Columns**, click **Before** or **After** to add a column on either side of the one your cursor is in. Click **Delete column** to remove the current column.

**Resize columns**

Point at the border between two columns until the cursor changes, then drag it left or right. One column gets wider and the one beside it narrower, so the table keeps its width. To make the whole table wider or narrower, drag its right edge; it stops at the edge of the page's text, or of the list item or table cell it sits in. Dragging needs a mouse or trackpad; on a touchscreen the columns keep their widths.

Click **Distribute columns** under **Columns** to give every column the same width.

**Add or remove rows**

Under **Rows**, click **Before** or **After** to add a row above or below the current one. Click **Delete row** to remove the current row.

**Merge and unmerge cells**

Select two or more cells, then click **Merge** under **Cells** to join them into one. Click **Unmerge** to divide a merged cell back into its original cells.

**Color cells**

Select one or more cells, then click the **Fill** row under **Cells** and pick a color. Choose **None** to take the color away. **Format → Table → Cell background color** does the same.

**Set cell borders**

Select one or more cells, then use the **Borders** row under **Cells**:

- **All** draws every border of the selected cells.
- **Outer** draws a box around the selection, with no lines inside it.
- **None** takes every border of the selected cells away.

To change the color, click the **Border** row and pick a color. The borders of the selected cells take it, and **All** and **Outer** draw in it. **Border** reads **Default** while the cells have the standard gray borders. Choosing **All** after **None** brings the gray borders back. **Format → Table** has **All borders**, **Outer borders**, and **No borders** too.

A row or column you add takes the borders and the color of the one beside it.

**Toggle the header row**

Click **Toggle header row** to switch the first row between a styled header row and a normal row.

**Delete the table**

Click **Delete table** at the bottom of the **Table** panel to remove the entire table from the document.
