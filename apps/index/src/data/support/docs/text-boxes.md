A text box holds paragraphs of its own in a box with a size, a fill, and an outline. Use one for a callout, a pull quote, or a label that sits on top of a picture or beside your text.

## Add a text box

1. Place your cursor where you want the box.
2. Open the **Insert** menu and choose **Text box**.
3. Type the box's text. It wraps inside the box, and the box grows to fit it.
4. Press **Esc** when you're done. The box stays selected, so you can change how it looks.

A new box sits on its own line, 6 cm wide, white with a thin black outline. **Text box** is grayed out in a header or footer, in a footnote, inside another text box, and in a code block. On a phone, **Insert** has no **Text box**.

## Edit the text in a box

Click the text in a box and type. The toolbar and the **Format** and **Insert** menus now work on the box's text, so you can make it bold, align a paragraph, or add a link. **⌘Z** on a Mac (**Ctrl+Z** on Windows and Linux) undoes your typing as it does in the rest of the document.

You can also select a box and press **Enter** to type at the end of its text. Press **Esc** to leave the box, and **Esc** again to put the cursor back in your text.

Formatting your document's text, such as making a whole paragraph bold, leaves the text in its boxes as it is.

## Move and resize a box

Click the edge of a box to select it. The edge is where you move it: drag it to another place. A box with no outline and no space around its text has no edge to click: click its text, then press **Esc** to select it. Once a box in front of or behind the text is selected, you can also drag it from anywhere inside it, and a click inside it puts you back in its text. A box on its own line moves between lines of text, and a box in front of or behind the text moves anywhere on the page.

Drag a handle on a corner or a side to change the box's size. The box never gets shorter than its text: the height you set is the least it takes, and the box grows when you type more.

## Change how a box looks

On a wide screen, selecting a box opens the **Text box** panel on the right side of the editor:

- **Width** and **Height**: type a size in centimeters. **Grow with text** is checked while the box takes the height of its text. Type a height to make it taller, and check **Grow with text** again to go back.
- **Style**: put the box on its own line, float it left or right with the text wrapping around it, or place it **Behind text** or **In front of text**.
- **Align**: when the box is on its own line, choose left, center, or right.
- **Fill** and **Outline**: pick a color, or **None**. With an outline, choose its **Weight** and its **Style**: **Solid**, **Dashed**, **Dotted**, or **Double**.
- **Rounded corners**: round the box's corners.
- **Horiz.** and **Vert.**: the space between the box's edges and its text, in centimeters.
- **Align** under **Text**: put the text at the top, the middle, or the bottom of a box that is taller than its text.

**Format → Text box** has the same styles and the **Fill**. It works on any screen, a phone included. On a phone you can select a box and change its style and fill, but not type in it.

A box behind the text sits under your text, which runs over it. Click where no text covers the box to type in it, or hold **Alt** (**Option** on a Mac) and click to select it.

## Good to know

- A box in a table can't go behind or in front of the text, so the panel and **Format → Text box** don't offer it there.
- The find bar searches the text in boxes, and **Tools → Word count** counts it.
- A comment on a box covers the whole box.
- A box copied and pasted into another document stays a box with its look.
- In the PDF and the web page a box looks as it does in the editor. In the Word file it's a Word text box. See [Export a document](/support/docs/export).
